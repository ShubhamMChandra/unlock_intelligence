# 2026-04-10 — Curriculum 2-day reframe brainstorm

**Context:** Brainstorm session with JT on whether the 8-module curriculum should be re-architected around a 2-day shape: Day 1 = personal unlock through guided build, Day 2 = matrix-driven systemic application. Used Dr. Kiran (instructor agent) for grilling, grounded against beta data from the 2026-04-10 instructor review and the two persona test rounds. Pre-implementation — shifts how the founding cohort gets designed and triggers a hard-deadline rewrite of Module 1 before the 2026-04-12 beta.

---

## The headline pivot

The 8-module / two-session curriculum (Session 1: Foundation & Fluency, Session 2: Strategy & Mastery) is being re-architected into a 2-day shape:

- **Day 1 — Personal unlock through build.** Each attendee builds one Project on their own real work. **Watchtower pattern:** a weekly read on the one external thing they should be tracking but aren't. Personalized starters per attendee. Canonical demo first, then 90-min build, then a second build pass for cadence, then structured share, then debrief.
- **Day 2 — Theoretical scaffolding through application.** Each attendee picks "the process they've been postponing" (personal or team, criterion is stakes), maps it across the matrix as a live build, then extracts 3–5 starter paragraphs for next builds. Walks out with a horizontal map and a pre-seeded queue.

Full implementation plan, block-by-block run-of-shows, and task list live in `.claude/plans/sorted-coalescing-phoenix.md`. This learning doc captures the reasoning so it survives.

## Validated pedagogical principles (the durable insights)

### 1. Matrix as lens, never as subject

The matrix is named on Day 1 as the punchline of the build ("what you just did touched all 5 of these") and used on Day 2 as the live-build tool for finding the next builds. It is never lectured. **Theory is delivered through doing, never through telling.**

**Why it matters:** Module 1's existing 5–8 min explicit teaching block has a pedagogical justification (load-bearing structural beam) but the "bigger demo, smaller attendee" pattern from round 2 (Priya's "kiddie version" quote, Jordan's "flex, not a promise") suggests instructor-driven framework explanation may actively reduce attendee agency. Filling beats teaching.

**Hard-deadline implication:** Module 1's matrix block is being rewritten before the 2026-04-12 beta. Same 5-min budget, flipped from explicit teaching to collaborative filling on the demo just watched.

### 2. Personalized starters Day 1, shared scaffold Day 2

The personalized starter + WHY annotation block was named by 6/6 round-2 personas and 5/5 round-1 personas as the strongest asset of Module 1. Free-form scaffold (Shubham's initial instinct in this brainstorm) was rejected because it gives up the trust device that has been beta-validated. Day 1 keeps personalization. Day 2 uses shared scaffold because the personalization has already happened.

**The principle:** "When the artifact is about you, you own it. When the artifact is about the pattern, you are learning a pattern." The trust device only works on Day 1, when the attendee is meeting the tool for the first time. By Day 2, the same person is ready to learn the pattern itself.

### 3. Success metric: Monday usage + the ability to find the next 10 Mondays without help

The narrower "Monday usage" framing was tested and then expanded. Pure Monday usage excludes the theoretical scaffolding that lets a skill generalize past Monday. Theory is allowed when it earns its keep through application that produces future builds. Theory loses its place when it becomes lecture slides nobody uses.

**Why it matters:** This is the disciplinary constraint that protects Day 2 from drifting into framework lecture (the failure mode Dr. Kiran flagged as the single biggest risk). It also resolves the Shubham/JT design conflict — Shubham's theory instinct is honored (scaffolding is real and load-bearing), JT's builder instinct is honored (every minute is hands-on).

### 4. Day 1 goal: each attendee produces, with their own hands, one output on their own real work that they could not have produced themselves

**Rejected:** "Show them it's real" as the Day 1 goal — it conflates instructor-spectacle WOW with attendee transfer. The beta data is unambiguous: Monday usage was driven by attendees seeing output on their own material with their own hands (Mira: "a machine gave me an actable choice"; Kasey: "that competitive intel thing on my actual account"). The bigger the instructor demo, the smaller the attendee feels. WOW is downstream of personal build, not upstream.

**JT's two unlocks** (the Day 1 promises): real effect + not that hard. Both non-negotiable.

## The Watchtower (Day 1 project vehicle)

Each attendee builds a Project that produces a weekly read on the one external thing they should be tracking but aren't. "Competitor" is reframed as "the regulator/partner/platform/team/market you can't ignore" — universal across roles.

**Why it beats the alternatives:**
- Cadence is built into the artifact (used Monday, the 8th, the 15th — not just once). Solves the round-2 cadence gap Priya and Mira both raised.
- Forces multi-step web search + synthesis + judgment about signal vs. noise. Genuinely agentic, not prompt-tricks.
- Every attendee's watchtower becomes a cell in their Day 2 matrix — clean Day 1 → Day 2 bridge.
- Universal across roles: Alan's market, Zeshawn's regulator, Jess's funder, Tommy's competitor, a GC's regulatory body.

**Pattern model:** One shared pattern (everyone builds a watchtower), personalized starters per attendee (each tailored to their actual external dependency). Easier to facilitate than fully different patterns; preserves the personalized-starter trust device.

---

## "The process you've been postponing" (Day 2 selection criterion)

Day 2 asks each attendee to pick the process they've been postponing — personal or team, the criterion is **stakes, not scope**. Same line Mira used in round 2. Forces honesty about what actually matters and dissolves the personal-vs-team scope debate entirely.

---

## Team design dynamic — Shubham theory, JT builder

Shubham is theory-leaning; JT is builder-leaning. This is a **healthy conflict, not a problem.** Shubham fights to keep skill from being a bag of tricks; JT fights to keep teaching from being a lecture series. Both forces are required. The Monday-usage-plus-future-builds metric is the structural protection that lets both win.

**Bias risk to manage:** Shubham's natural pull toward theory + frameworks means Day 2 is the day where the curriculum is most likely to drift into lecture. JT becomes the enforcement mechanism. The "every Day 2 block produces a Monday-usable artifact" rule is the discipline.

## What got rejected and why

| Rejected | Why | Replaced by |
|---|---|---|
| 8-module / Session 1+2 frame | Module 1 alone takes 75 min — there isn't 4-modules-of-runway in Session 1 | 2-day reframe with one build per day |
| Free-form scaffold both days | Throws away the personalized-starter trust device for instructor-cost reasons | Personalized starters Day 1, shared scaffold Day 2 |
| Matrix as Day-2-only deep dive | Day 1 of pure vertical work hardens the vertical mental model the program is supposed to fight | Matrix as lens on Day 1, live build tool on Day 2 |
| "Show them it's real" as Day 1 goal | Conflates instructor spectacle with attendee transfer; bigger demo → smaller attendee | Personal-build-as-unlock (real effect + not that hard) |
| Meeting prep / morning brief / inbox triage / doc-to-decision as Day 1 project | Not basic — under-ambitious in the same category. None had built-in cadence. | Watchtower (cadence built into the artifact) |
| Day 2 = strategy / 90-day roadmap / portfolio scoring | Strategy doesn't get used Monday; 90-day roadmaps don't survive the meeting | Day 2 = matrix as live build + extract next builds |
| Day 2 pair pressure-test at 30 min | Most cuttable beat under time pressure | 10-min version: "share one thing you noticed about your map" |
| Lecture-format matrix block in Module 1 | "The matrix cannot be a lecture. Either matrix work is a build or it is dead." | Live matrix fill on the demo — same time budget, flipped from teaching to building |

## What's parked for deeper discussion

These came up in the brainstorm but don't get resolved here. Each needs its own session. Captured in `knowledge/ideas/backlog.md` so they don't get lost.

1. **Manager stretch positioning.** Whether the program serves managers and ICs in one cohort, splits into two products, caps the manager mix, or sells a manager-only add-on. Until resolved, Day 2 is designed for IC consumption only.
2. **Schedule shape pressure test.** 8-hour budget is soft, not hard ("felt right, what we could sustain"). Test 2×4h vs 3×2.5h vs 1×6–7h vs flipped-density 2×4h after the founding cohort runs once.
3. **Audience demographic check for the founding cohort.** JT's two unlocks (real effect + not that hard) assume a senior-skeptical audience. Need to walk the actual signup list before locking in personalized watchtower starters.

Right after the demo lands, instructor pulls up the 5 columns and the room collaboratively fills them in for the demo they just watched. Touches:

## What worked in the brainstorm process itself

- **Dr. Kiran (instructor agent) grilling against beta data** was the highest-leverage move. The agent had access to the persona test rounds and the instructor review and used them to push back with specific quotes ("kiddie version," "actable choice"). Generic teaching wisdom would have produced generic answers.
- **Multiple AskUserQuestion rounds with explicit recommendations + "Other"** gave Shubham room to correct the framing in real time ("8 hour budget is not hard constraint but felt right") and to push back on overshoots ("skills need a scaffolding of theory").
- **Naming the team dynamic out loud** (Shubham theory, JT builder) converted what could have been a hidden bias into a structural feature of the design process.
- **The grill-then-synthesize loop**: Dr. Kiran grilled, the user pushed back, the assistant synthesized, the user pushed back again. The synthesis ("Monday usage + ability to find next 10 Mondays") was better than either pole alone.

## What didn't work

- **First round of project options** (meeting prep, morning brief, doc-to-decision, inbox triage) was rejected as basic. The fix: raise ambition inside the category, not change category. Dr. Kiran caught this on the second pass.
- **Initial "Monday usage" hard line** was too narrow and excluded the theoretical scaffolding Shubham correctly insisted on. Had to be expanded to "Monday usage + next 10 Mondays."
- **Initial "matrix as lens, never as subject"** framing may have over-rotated against explicit teaching. The 2026-04-12 beta will tell us whether the live-fill approach actually outperforms the explicit-teaching approach. Hedge: keep the explicit-teaching version available in case we need to revert.
