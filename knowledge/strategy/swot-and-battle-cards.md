## SWOT

### Strengths

- **Dual credential nobody can match.** The instructor teaches at UChicago (75+ students, direct lesson planning) and builds AI workflows at Digital Realty ($40B+ data center REIT). No one else in the market does both.
- **8-hour format hits the sweet spot.** Half-day workshops are too shallow to change behavior. Multi-week programs are too slow for busy teams. ILT completion rates run 85-95% vs. 5-15% for self-paced. We're in the only gap worth occupying.
- **Three deliverables the buyer's boss can see on Monday.** AI integration blueprint, workflow automation map, 90-day strategy. No competitor under $3K produces custom artifacts.
- **$1,295 founding sits inside the typical professional development budget** ($1-2K/yr). No CFO escalation required for individual seats.
- **Process-first methodology.** We teach horizontal workflow redesign, not "how to use ChatGPT." This is harder to copy because it requires real operational experience.
- **Chicago-local.** In-person option when national competitors are online-only. Geographic trust signal for Midwest enterprise buyers.
- **Lean cost structure.** No campus overhead, no full-time faculty. Can price aggressively while maintaining margin.

### Weaknesses

- **Zero social proof.** No testimonials, no case studies, no alumni count. Pre-launch credibility rests on instructor credentials alone.
- **Two-person team.** Single instructor = no schedule redundancy, no parallel cohorts, capacity ceiling of ~10-15 cohorts/year.
- **No brand recognition.** "Unlock Intelligence" has no search volume. HR buyers default to known brands when unsure.
- **No enterprise sales infrastructure.** No CRM, no SDR, no procurement-ready SOW. Every deal is hand-sold.
- **Curriculum not yet market-validated.** Founding cohort is the first live test. Refund risk exists.

### Opportunities

- **86% of companies increasing AI budgets in 2026.** Budget exists; the problem is finding quality training, not money.
- **82% invest in AI training but 59% still report a skills gap.** Existing solutions are failing. Buyers are actively looking for something that works.
- **Self-paced platform fatigue is real.** "Your Coursera subscription isn't working" lands because L&D teams already know their completion rates are terrible.
- **No dominant player in the $1-3K live-workshop-for-non-technical-teams niche.** Teamland is closest but no deliverables. Revenue AI has no geographic focus.
- **Harvard validates our exact format** (live, half-day, no-code) at $2,850+. Upmarket pricing air cover.
- **$9,750/year per employee in recoverable productivity.** ROI case writes itself -- program pays back in month one.

### Threats

- **UChicago Professional Ed could launch a short-format program.** They have the brand, the alumni network, and the infrastructure. This would neutralize the credential advantage.
- **General Assembly is launching 15 new AI courses in 2026.** If they release a 1-day corporate format, they bring brand + sales engine we can't match today.
- **"Just give everyone ChatGPT" is a real CFO objection.** AI tools getting easier to use reduces perceived need for structured training.
- **Low barriers to entry.** Any consultant with AI experience can launch a competing workshop. The market gets noisier from here.
- **22+ hour corporate procurement cycles** with no case studies to accelerate them means deals will move slowly at this stage.

## Top 5 Objection Handlers

### 1. "Is 8 hours really enough?"

**What's behind it:** They've been burned by shallow workshops. Afraid of buying something too thin.

**Response:** Don't defend the hours -- reframe around output. "We don't measure this in hours, we measure it in deliverables. Your team leaves with an AI integration blueprint, a workflow automation map, and a 90-day strategy. That's more tangible output than most 8-week programs produce. Eight hours is just how long it takes to build them."

**Proof point:** Harvard runs a similar live half-day format at $2,850+ and considers it sufficient for executive AI fluency.

### 2. "We already have Coursera / LinkedIn Learning."

**What's behind it:** The L&D budget already went somewhere. They need justification for additional spend.

**Response:** "What's the completion rate on those courses? Industry average is 5-15%. That's not your team's fault -- self-paced doesn't work for skill transformation. This fills a different need: live, team-based, deliverable-focused. You're not replacing your platform. You're activating the team that isn't using it."

**Proof point:** 82% of companies invest in AI training, 59% still report a skills gap. The spend isn't working.

### 3. "Can't we just give everyone ChatGPT and let them figure it out?"

**What's behind it:** Budget pressure from a CFO or ops lead.

**Response:** "Giving everyone a tool without teaching them how to think about where it fits is how you get 100 people using AI as a fancy Google. The gap isn't access -- it's knowing which workflows to redesign. Your average knowledge worker wastes 2.5 hours a week on tasks AI could handle. At $75 an hour, that's nearly $10K per person per year sitting on the table."

**Proof point:** $9,750/year per employee in recoverable productivity. Program ROI in month one.

### 4. "Who else has done this program?"

**What's behind it:** Risk aversion. Corporate buyers don't want to be first.

**Response:** "We're running our founding cohort this spring -- which is exactly why the price is $1,295 instead of $1,995. You get the same instructor who teaches 75+ students at UChicago, the same curriculum, the same deliverables -- at a founding rate that goes away. The instructor isn't new to this; just the company is."

**Proof point:** UChicago teaching track record (75+ students, direct lesson planning). Digital Realty enterprise AI deployment.

### 5. "I need to run this by my VP / CFO."

**What's behind it:** Not an objection -- this is the buying process. The champion is sold but needs ammunition.

**Response:** "Absolutely -- I'll send you a one-pager you can forward. It covers the ROI case, program structure, and instructor credentials. What would your VP need to see to say yes? Let me make sure it's in there."

**Proof point:** Arm them with the $9,750/year productivity number, the "8 hours not 8 weeks" comparison, and the founding cohort pricing deadline.
