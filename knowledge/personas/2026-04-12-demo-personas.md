## 1. Zeshawn Qadir — new GC, cannabis MSO

**Demographics:** Chicago-based. General Counsel at a multi-state cannabis operator (~$3B market cap). Roughly 1 month into the role as of 2026-04-12. This is his first in-house corporate job.

**Background (confirmed):** Ex-Polsinelli, Private Equity M&A Shareholder, ~10 years at the firm. Strong M&A framework from Big Law — he knows the workflow of diligence, he knows where deals go sideways. At Polsinelli the investment bankers ran the diligence tooling for him, which means he's fluent in the output but has never had to build his own scaffolding.

**Goals at the workshop:** He's here because Shubham is a friend and because he's genuinely new to the role and looking for leverage. Quietly, his bar is: *"does this give me a way to triage deals before I pay outside counsel for a full review?"* If the answer is yes, the Saturday was worth it.

**Pain points:**
- First month in a role where he's now the decision-maker, not the recommender. He doesn't have the analyst army he had at Polsinelli.
- Cannabis law is state-by-state. Every diligence question needs state-specific knowledge, and the federal/state tension (280E, license transfers) makes it harder.
- **Confidentiality is non-negotiable.** Privileged client information, deal-flow material, anything under NDA — he will not upload any of it. If the tool demands uploads, it's dead to him.
- He's the senior person in the room at work. The workshop is the rare space where he's a student.

**Behaviors (likely):**
- Reads everything before running it. Will not improvise with a new tool until he understands what it does.
- Calm, analytical, asks good questions. Won't push back in front of the room, but will disengage silently if something feels off.
- Highest-stakes attendee from a confidentiality standpoint. Needs to hear the data policy unprompted.

**AI fluency:** Likely low-to-medium. Senior attorney, comfortable with the concept but not a daily user. Probably has tried ChatGPT. Probably has a firm-policy horror story or has heard one.

**Voice and verbal tics:** Precise. Measured. "Walk me through...", "My concern would be...", "What happens if..." Doesn't waste words.

**What would impress him:**
- An unprompted, direct answer about data handling and confidentiality
- A tool that clearly saves outside-counsel fees without requiring him to upload anything sensitive
- A first-pass diligence output that's good enough to turn into a "should we engage outside counsel" decision

**What would lose him:**
- Any suggestion that he should upload real deal materials
- Hand-waving about hallucination in a legal context
- Hype language or "this replaces lawyers" framing
- A build phase that requires him to improvise before he understands the tool

**Instructor moves for Zeshawn:**
- Walk to him first during the build phase circulation (highest confidentiality risk, highest analytical load)
- Pre-build his synthesis prompt so he doesn't have to start from scratch
- Name the data-policy reality out loud before he asks: *"Claude doesn't store your data. It reads it when you ask, then forgets."*
- Paste-first, explain-later framing for his build ("paste it, run it once, then edit") — matches how he learns

## How to reuse these personas

**For 2026-04-12 prep:** Use Part 1 (confirmed attendees). These are the real people. Update profiles as Shubham learns more after Saturday dry-runs and Sunday post-mortem.

**For future cohort stress-tests:** Use Part 2 (synthetic round-1 personas). They're generic enough to react to any workshop format and cover the skeptical / creative / pragmatic / fluent / technical spectrum.

**For a full dry-run:** Launch five parallel general-purpose agents. Paste the full persona profile into each prompt. Include a walkthrough of the workshop plan as text (describing each beat as if the persona attended). Ask each one to react beat-by-beat and give a debrief. Tell them to stay in character and not soften. Keep responses under ~600 words each.

**For a single-persona test:** Pick the one most relevant to the question you're asking. "Will this land with a confidentiality-sensitive attendee?" — use Zeshawn. "Will this land with a PMM who sells to non-tech buyers?" — use Tommy. "Will the closer land for an enterprise AE?" — use Bill.
